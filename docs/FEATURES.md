Already faster than the pull? It turns you toward the hook instead of adding speed (gravity on a swing still can). Hooking from the floor pops you up. Near the hook the pull stops and you coast in. | Grappling Hook (projectile hook) | working, wants a playtest | The only grappling hook (2026-11-19: it replaced the first, instant-hit hook, and took its name; a saved choice of the old `grapple2` id is this hook). The hook is a projectile, not an instant hit: it flies at 85 m/s along your aim (half a second to the end of its 45 m), and you run, jump and shoot freely meanwhile. The rope pays out behind it with slack (1 m plus 8 cm per metre flown): it sags and a corkscrew of ripples runs down it. When the hook bites, nothing pulls yet: the winch takes the slack in, faster and faster (under 0.2 s), sooner if you move away and later if you move toward it; then the rope snaps tight with a twang and a yank (9.8 m/s toward the hook), and from there it pulls and swings as below. Let go in flight to call it back; a miss comes back and costs the cooldown. **The way back** (2026-11-18): let go or missed, the hook really comes back to your wrist (about 110 m/s, 0.12 to 0.45 s, straight at you wherever you have got to), and while you hold the button it catches the first surface in its way, with slack to take in and a yank as after any bite: miss into the sky, drop behind a ledge, and it snags the ledge; or let go, then press again and hold as it comes back. Nothing nearer than 2.5 m, and a jump off the rope does not count as holding on (the hook just comes back). **In first person the rope always starts on your arm's wrist**: it used to be drawn from where the arm was a frame earlier, so it came loose from the arm as soon as you moved or turned (34 cm at 13 m/s); the arm also stays out until the hook is back. Aim magnets work at the moment of firing (a grapple point is flown to, a player is led by their speed), and in flight the hook catches a player it passes within 0.7 m of. HUD ring: a spinning broken orange ring while the hook is out. Its own sound when the rope pulls tight. Bots fire it with lead and hold on through the flight. **In step everywhere**: the shot is a short record of moments (fired, stopped, pulled tight, let go) that rides on every snapshot while it is out; every copy of the game, spectators included, rebuilds it and draws the hook and rope from that record alone at the same moment it draws that player's body, so a lost or late packet changes nothing and the hook stops on the wall on every screen; the killcam records what was drawn and draws it again. `Grapple` (physics), `HookLine` (the record), `HookRope` (the rope's shape); `tests/grappling_hook_test.gd`, `tests/run_hook_sync_test.sh` |
| Wall-run, wall-jump | working | Hold forward along a wall in the air. Low gravity for 1.6 s, camera leans. Jump to kick off. Same wall can't be reused until you land |
| Vault, ledge climb | working | Run into anything 0.45 to 1.25 m high and you vault it, keeping speed. Jump at a ledge up to 2.3 m and you climb up |
| Dive and roll | working | Q (or Alt): lunge toward where you look (+4 m/s, low hitbox), from the ground or once per jump, 1 s cooldown. Landing from a dive rolls you forward keeping your speed; hold crouch to roll into a slide |
| Belly slide | working | Keep holding dive as you land and you slide along on your front instead of rolling (+1 m/s on impact, a little less friction than a slide, weak steering, speeds up downhill). Lowest hitbox in the game (0.8 m) and you can shoot the whole way. Let go (or jump) to get up; let go while holding crouch to go straight into a slide. If the slide runs out while you are still holding dive you crawl (2 m/s), except on a slope steeper than about 10 degrees, where you keep sliding down it. Under a gap too low to crouch in you stay down until you crawl out |
| Glide | working | The **bullet glide** (2026-11-19). Hold Alt in mid-air (rebindable; it takes a press in the air, and not within 0.5 m of the floor): your rise or fall eases out in about 0.2 s and you fly on along a locked track, the way you were going at the speed you had: no boost, no slowdown, and no steering (WASD and the camera never bend it), free to turn, look and shoot anywhere. From a standstill you just hang in place. **Hold it as long as you like, but it weakens:** you hardly sink for the first ~1.5 s, then the fall builds until, 4 s in, it is a plain fall (still on the track, gravity in full from there). The seconds add up over one time in the air, so letting go and pressing again carries on where it was (no fresh float); landing, a wall run or the grapple start it afresh, and GLIDE on the HUD drains as it weakens. **Guns scatter half as much while you glide** (the crosshair tightens to show it). Shooting and melee swings work while gliding; a flip, dive, grapple, wall run or ground slam takes over from it. **Rocket boots while gliding** do not end it: the burst sends the track off the way you press (WASD as you face; forward with nothing held), with all the speed you had plus the burst's. A hard shove knocks you out of it; a bullet only nudges you along. **Looks:** a snappy launch into a sleek, stretched-out flying pose, the hips tipped into the wind along the track and the legs together trailing in line, toes pointed, while the upper body follows your aim (the chest leans into the flight only when you aim down the track); as it weakens the body tips nose-down and the legs start to kick. Let go in mid-air after 0.4 s or more of gliding and you **backflip out of it**: one long, open flip (about a second) straight out of the flying pose and over the line of the track, the back arched and the arms flung wide over the top with one knee up and the other leg long behind, a quick tuck through the last of the turn, then out into the fall (looks only: no lift, no speed change, and it does not use your Space flip). A shot or swing hurries it to its end so the arms can aim, the floor coming up hurries it too so it lands on its feet, and any other move cuts it short. In first person your view never flips (your arms tuck in with it). A thin wind trail off each foot in your trim colour and a few faint speed streaks past you, a looping wind that rises in pitch and volume with your speed, a whoosh on the backflip, and in first person the view widens a little when you are fast. Everyone sees and hears it. Bots use it (race bots at glide gaps, arena bots on boost crossings). Numbers in movement_tuning.gd (Glide), GLIDE_SPREAD in player.gd, the look at the top of robot_animator.gd, the trail in glide_fx.gd; filmed by `tools/robot/glide_film.sh` |
| Flips (air flip) | working | Press jump again in mid-air: a small lift upward (at least 4.5 m/s up) to bridge a longer gap, with no push along and no speed kept; WASD only picks which way the body turns over (others see the flip; in first person the view, arms and legs stay as in a plain jump). Once per jump (refreshed by wall runs and the grapple). A jump pressed just above the floor still counts as a bunny-hop |

## Known issues

- Animations have only been judged from rendered still frames so far, not in motion on a
  real PC. Timing and feel will need a playtest pass.
- The muzzle flashes are still simple placeholders.
- The sound effects have only been checked by measurement and automated tests, not
  listened to on a real PC yet: expect to tune levels in `game/audio/sfx_list.gd`.
  That goes double for Halcyon's low gravity chord and terminal hum, which are meant
  to set a mood.
- Empyrean Tower has only been run in a software renderer, automated tests and headless bot
  matches, never by a person on a graphics card or online with friends: its frame rate on real
  hardware (15,300 blocks, the most of any map, plus 250 boxes of shader-driven magic), how the low gravity outside feels and the lifts
  all want a playtest.
- Cirrus has only been run in a software renderer, automated tests and short headless
  bot games, never on a real graphics card or with people. Its frame rate, how a fall
  and a ride feel, whether 8 s is enough warning from deep inside a building, and its
  sounds (above all how loud the tick is) need checking on a PC. Bots getting off
  doomed islands was only seen at one change.
- Halcyon has only been run in a software renderer and in headless bot matches, never
  on a real graphics card: its frame rate, and how the blue event lighting reads in a
  real fight, need checking on a PC.
- The sniper rifle has no scope or zoom. Right mouse is free now (the mobility tool moved
  to middle mouse), so one could go there.
- No LAN address detection on some networks: the lobby's code may stand for 127.0.0.1 (the
  lobby shows the address too). Use `ipconfig` to find your address and join with that if so.
- Lobby codes hold an IPv4 address only; a host reachable only over IPv6 needs the address typed.
